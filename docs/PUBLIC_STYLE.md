# Public style

Applies to Sonzai and Mind Layer websites, demos, README introductions, launch posts,
video scripts, captions, screenshots, and future public materials. User direction,
September 12, 2026: less information, more direct copy, and readable editorial type.

## Say what it does

Explain the product in one or two connected sentences. Name the action and its
result. Carry useful information in the prose rather than scattering it across
badges, eyebrow labels, tooltips, cards, and slogan fragments. Use concrete examples.

Use “Mind Layer stores facts your agent can retrieve in later conversations.”
Avoid “A new conversation. A shared history. A little place to grow.”
Use “Chat with Moss” for an action and “Memory and personality” for an inspector.
Avoid “A moment together” and “Go inside the magic.”

Lead with one purpose and one primary action. Remove duplicate links and repeated
explanations. Keep cost, privacy, and live-versus-scripted distinctions beside the
action they affect. Put secondary setup and reference material in clearly named
sections; preserve complete technical, legal, and evidence records.

## Make it readable

Use a Substack-like editorial reading experience, not a copy of its branding.
Use Charter with Cambria/Georgia fallbacks for prose and headings; system sans for
navigation, buttons, labels, and status; monospace for code. Prefer local fonts.
Use 18–20px reading text, about 1.6 line height, and a 65–72 character column.
Headings should fit the content rather than fill the viewport.

Use warm white, dark text, one restrained accent, simple borders, and generous
spacing. Avoid gradients, glow, fake paper texture, decorative counters, floating
badges, oversized slogans, and repeated boxed benefit lists. Keep illustration
where it is the product experience: Moss can have a greenhouse without the whole
page pretending to be a wooden noticeboard. Animation should communicate state
or support the character, and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Show the product

For Moss, show the character, a short explanation, its response, and the interaction
choices. Make chat, history, and technical inspection available without overwhelming
the first screen. Trait feedback must reflect stored game state, never invented XP.
For websites, use a short introduction, a real demo, and direct setup links.

Write as someone explaining their work. Do not invent testimonials, results,
customer counts, motivations, or experiences. Preserve labels on scripted scenes,
synthetic data, edited recordings, and model-generated answers. The standalone
Mind Layer and hosted Sonzai platform have different features and API contracts.

## Technical writing inspiration

Use the clarity of an OpenAI technical product article: state the problem and
what changed, explain the mechanism, show an example, and describe the limits.
Keep Sonzai’s own voice and attribution. This is a structural reference, not an
endorsement or permission to copy OpenAI branding or claims.

Reference: [Introducing Structured Outputs in the API](https://openai.com/index/introducing-structured-outputs-in-the-api/).
Its useful pattern is a concrete developer problem, a working example, an
under-the-hood explanation, and explicit limitations. Apply only the depth the
reader needs; a homepage is shorter than a technical guide.

Write in complete sentences with clear actors: “Your application stores a fact”
and “Mind Layer retrieves it for a later reply.” Explain why a detail matters
before naming the implementation. Use factual first-person plural for work the
team did. Avoid breathless announcements, vague claims of intelligence, invented
traction, and implying that every demo result is guaranteed.

Example:

> A model can use an earlier conversation only when your application supplies
> the context. Mind Layer stores facts and retrieves relevant memories for a
> later reply. The demo lets you inspect those memories alongside recent turns.

## Apply and check

Use this standard whenever creating or editing public-facing material. Check the
opening aloud, desktop and phone layouts, keyboard navigation, contrast, links,
and the real interaction path. Rebuild generated artifacts from their source.
Existing recordings remain evidence of the build they show; use this treatment
for new recordings rather than passing changed mockups off as original footage.
